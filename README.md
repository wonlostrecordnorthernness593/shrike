## 🚀 What Is shrike?

shrike is a click-through debugger and visibility tool. In everyday language, it is like a tracking device for a single action inside your app. When you click something, shrike records the path that action takes and shows it in a way you can follow.

Instead of guessing why a button did not work, why a page showed the wrong value, or why data did not arrive where you expected, you can watch the actual trip. You see the stops, the values, and the order of events.

The name “shrike” comes from a bird known for watching closely and acting with precision. That is the idea here: watch closely, see the real path, and fix the real problem.

A simple example: a customer says a discount code is not working. With a normal log file, you might see a line that says the code was rejected. With shrike, you see the code the customer typed, the value it was compared against, the moment the comparison happened, and the answer that came back. The difference between those two experiences is the whole point of the tool.

Another example: a page shows the wrong total. shrike lets you walk backward through the trail and see the number as it existed at each step. Somewhere between the database and the screen, the number changed. shrike shows you where.

## 🖱️ Your First Click-Through

Once shrike is open on your computer, the first session is short and calm. You do not need to prepare anything complicated.

Start by opening shrike and looking at the main window. You will see a starting area where you choose what to watch. Point shrike at the app you want to look inside, then go back to that app and click the thing you are curious about.

Return to shrike. A trail should now appear. At the top is where you started. Below it are the stops in order. Each stop shows what happened there and which values were present.

Click any stop to open it up. You will see more detail: the moment it occurred, what came in, what went out, and how long it took. If a value looks wrong, you have found the place to focus on.

If the trail is long, do not try to read it all. Start at the end and work backward. The last stop is where the result appeared. The stop before it is where the result was created. The one before that is where its ingredients came from. Following the chain backward is the fastest way to find the faulty link.

Save the trail when you find something useful. A saved trail is easy to share with a teammate or an AI helper, and it contains the real values rather than a description of them.

## 🤖 Built to Work With AI Helpers

Modern debugging often involves an AI assistant. shrike is designed for that world from the start.

When you share a trail with an AI helper, the helper is not reading your description of the problem. It is reading the same stops, values, and order of events that you are looking at. That means its suggestions are based on the real evidence.

This is a meaningful change. Instead of an assistant guessing at a likely cause, it can point at a specific stop and explain what went wrong there. The conversation becomes shorter and the fix becomes more accurate. Support teams can hand a trail to an assistant and get a grounded answer. Developers can hand the same trail back and skip the long explanation.
